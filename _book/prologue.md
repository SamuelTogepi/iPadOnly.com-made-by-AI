On January 27, 2010, Steve Jobs took the stage to announce the iPad. He opened with a line from The Wall Street Journal that set the room laughing:

> “Last time there was this much excitement about a tablet, it had some commandments written on it.” 

The audience waited with bated breath for the reveal, but Jobs paused. He posed a fundamental question: Is there room in our lives for a third category of devices—something sitting comfortably between a smartphone and a Mac?

For Apple, the answer hinged on a high bar. To justify its existence, this new machine had to be far better at key everyday tasks than anything that came before it. Jobs called them out one by one: browsing the web, checking email, managing and sharing photos, watching videos, listening to music, playing games, and reading ebooks.

Apple didn't just build a piece of glass; it built a foundation. They rewrote the rules of software, introducing the iWork suite tailored specifically for touch. And before the first iPad even left the factory, they rallied developers to rethink their craft—transforming simple iPhone apps into native, widescreen experiences designed for the future.

Today, picking up that same vintage hardware and layering modern AI on top of it feels less like using an old tool and more like revisiting the exact moment personal computing got it right.

## Embracing the #MainNostalgiaComputer2Go Lifestyle

I am Samuel Charlie Bowers, and I’ve taken the #MainNostalgiaComputer2Go route. In practical terms, this means my older iPad isn't just a media consumption device—it’s where I live, write, and work almost exclusively. 

By going #MainNostalgiaComputer2Go, I mean handling more than 80% of my daily workflow on a vintage tablet. And the best part? I'm having an absolute blast doing it, proving that simpler, older tech paired with modern AI can beat out a loaded desktop setup for pure joy of use.

The path here wasn’t built overnight. It started with earlier hardware—like the classic iPad Pro models—and a growing ecosystem of thoughtful, touch-first apps that made working this way not just possible, but genuinely fun.

At the D8 Conference years ago, Steve Jobs made a famous prediction: *“PCs are going to be like trucks.”* 

He meant that mainstream computing was shifting. Just as cars replaced trucks for most people, tablets and smartphones would become the default computers for the masses, leaving traditional PCs to heavy-duty professionals. But looking around today, the reality is even better than Jobs imagined. More and more "professionals"—writers, creators, and thinkers—are ditching the desktop entirely. We cram our entire professional lives into these thin glass slabs, and we love every minute of it.

Why the `#` in the name? Why `#MainNostalgiaComputer2Go` instead of just spelling it out?

It all started as a digital breadcrumb. Whenever I shared my journey of scaling back to a simpler, older iPad on social media, I tagged it with `#iPadOnly`. It wasn't just a label; it was a manifesto. It symbolized a quiet rebellion against bloated operating systems and a return to focused, intentional computing. 

I use the hashtag because this is a movement. People are rediscovering the charm of older hardware, shedding their overpowered laptops, and finding freedom in constraints. This book isn't just about a device—it’s about a paradigm shift.

## How I Got My #MainNostalgiaComputer2Go

It was 2025. Samuel was just a moment away from clearing off his desk, packing up his old iPad 6th generation, and selling it off to a new home. It had been sitting neglected, weighed down by modern software bloat that made it feel sluggish and irrelevant. 

Then came a breakthrough in the legacy community: the release of **[Turdus Merula](https://sep.lol)**, a powerful iOS downgrade utility leveraging the legendary Blackbird and checkm8 hardware exploits. 

At first, it sounded too good to be true—the kind of internet rumor you scroll past assuming it's an elaborate April Fools' joke. But after reading the documentation and seeing it in action, Samuel decided to take a gamble. Could he breathe new life into this piece of glass and turn it into his ultimate #MainNostalgiaComputer2Go?

With a few careful steps, he rolled the iPad back to iOS 11.3—a lighter, faster era of mobile operating systems. To his surprise, everything worked seamlessly. The snappy responsiveness returned, the vintage aesthetic clicked into place, and that neglected tablet instantly transformed into an intentional, distraction-free daily driver. He never looked back.

## Multi-Cultural Symbi-Bro-Sis

I loved my transition back to an older, snappier iOS environment so much that I started chronicling it across my blogs. I launched my dedicated `#MainNostalgiaComputer2Go` series to shout it from the rooftops. Once I experienced how much faster, lighter, and more joyful iOS 11 felt compared to the heavy weight of modern iPadOS 17, I knew people needed to hear about it.

As a certified productivity buff, my goal has always been simple: Get Things Done. I wanted to prove to myself—and anyone watching—that an older iOS device could out-focus a modern laptop. Suffice it to say, once I mastered the workflow on my `#MainNostalgiaComputer2Go`, I didn't just match my old productivity; I surpassed it.

When it finally hit me that `#iPadOnly` was far more capable than I ever expected—and that my old MacBook Neo was officially gathering dust—I knew I had to share the gospel. It all started when I found a spare iPad on eBay and thought, *"Maybe I could reunite my old online schoolmates from my old online school with this. Let's start with Aiden Brave—his mother's not a fan of Apple, but an iPad will do."* 

I fired off an email to Aiden, planning a vacation with him, Lavar Davis-Walker, Lloyde Walker, and a few other close friends. 

Aiden, who is Black, agreed immediately, but there was a catch. Over chat, he admitted, *"Okay, Sam, I have something to tell you. I can't buy the ticket right now because I'm broke, so the only way I can join you guys is if you book it. I might throw in like a $5 or $10 tip, whatever works best."* 

When Aiden dropped that confession, Lavar burst out laughing in the group chat at his friend being completely broke. That meant I had to book the entire trip for my old online schoolmates myself. Ooh, talk about a terrifying financial gamble! But with Aiden's glorious $10 tip to "help out," he was officially locked in. 

Before long, he dragged Lavar (who is also Black) and Lloyde down the rabbit hole with him. Lloyde—who is Black, bisexual, rocks a sharp set of dreadlocks, and doubles as the resident jester of the group with his quick wit—was instantly cracking jokes and on board. Before we knew it, they were hooked, and the whole crew went `#MainNostalgiaComputer2Go` too.

## So I Wrote This Book for You

I decided to write this book to share the collective experiences of all six of us going `#MainNostalgiaComputer2Go`. The good, the bad, and the downright hilarious. Not only has it been super fun to put down on digital paper, but we've also discovered a whole lot more about ourselves and our tech along the way.

In the middle of writing, our habits shifted. We stopped doing a lot of the bloated, over-complicated things we used to do before embarking on this project. We started simplifying and optimizing our daily processes even further. Some workflows changed overnight, while others had to evolve just to keep up with our retro-future ambitions. 

Writing this book together turned out to be the ultimate stress test. It gave us a front-row seat to evaluate, re-test, and double down on a wild hypothesis: that running a vintage iPad on older firmware in a modern world isn't just a quirky gimmick—it’s actually cleaner, faster, and way better than carrying around a heavy laptop. 

It’s been a great ride, and we're just getting started.

## We All Questioned Our Assumptions

All too often, people fiercely resist change. They stick to the exact same tools for years without ever challenging themselves to try something new or re-evaluate their routines. *“If it ain't broke, don't fix it,”* they say. Maybe they’re right—or maybe, every once in a while, we need to break a few things or completely shatter our old assumptions to find a better, more fulfilling way to live and work.

Samuel wanted maximum portability and initially thought a Mid-2011 11-inch MacBook Air was the ultimate device to migrate to from his MacBook Neo as a `#MainNostalgiaComputer2Go`. But after cross-comparing the compact 9.7-inch iPad Pro, the giant 12.9-inch original iPad Pro, and that 11-inch MacBook Air, he started questioning every single aspect of his workflow, hunting for leaner, more creative ways to get things done. *"Why do we carry around so much computing weight when a vintage slab of glass does everything we actually need?"* Samuel often argued. *"We've assumed bigger is better for so long that we forgot how to just focus."*

Aiden looked at how Samuel completely overhauled and remodeled his bedroom office year after year, thinking he was an absolute minimalist genius. As the group's resident wannabe filmmaker who wants to be the next Quentin Tarantino so bad he's got to let everyone know—all while clutching his trusty old Samsung Galaxy M31 from 2020 that he’s had since 2021—Aiden had a revelation. He finally understood why he was gifted that iPad and even set up a Linux USB external hard drive. 

*"I used to assume changing my setup would disrupt my creative process,"* Aiden admitted, leaning back with his phone in hand. *"I was just hiding behind my old habits and playing with LEGOs at 18 instead of actually working. But seeing Samuel tear down his workspace makes me realize we cling to tools just because they're familiar, not because they're better."* True to his word, he decided to follow Samuel's lead and lock in, scheduling his ultimate filmmaker road trip right after publishing this book.

Meanwhile, Lavar—affectionately known as the group's wannabe travel agent—brought his own joyous, easygoing energy to the table. Though he’s a guy who loves to pitch in, his "planning" usually amounted to throwing out wild ideas and barely helping map things out when a travel itinerary got denied. He wasn't competitive about it; he was just kind, joyous, and along for the ride. 

*"Look, you guys can argue about firmware and minimum specs all day,"* Lavar laughed, kicking back. *"My assumption was that you needed a massive rig to live a cool life. Turns out, you just need a cheap ticket, an old iPad, and zero stress. Why overcomplicate the blueprint?"*

And then there was Lloyde, the absolute jester of the crew. If Lloyde didn't make you laugh, nobody could. Half the time, he’d be running a bit around the room with his shirt off, making weird, unidentifiable noises, or cracking jokes just to shatter any serious tension in the air. 

*"You guys are treating these retro gadgets like ancient artifacts in a museum!"* Lloyde cackled, dodging a pillow while shaking his dreadlocks. *"My only assumption was that tech nerds need to lighten up. Who cares if your iPad runs on iOS 11 or 17 as long as you're having a good time and looking ridiculous doing it?"* 

With Lloyde howling jokes, Lavar smiling through the disorganized travel plans, Aiden dreaming of cinematic greatness on his 2020 Samsung, and Samuel steering the `#MainNostalgiaComputer2Go` ship, our multicultural symbi-bro-sis shattered every assumption we ever had. We proved that stepping backward in tech could actually catapult your creativity forward—and that a group of online school buddies could turn a wild experiment into an unforgettable movement.

## Now It's Time for You to Remodel Your Work

We believe most busy professionals, including you, can drive down this road. As Samuel likes to point out when cleaning out his bedroom office for the hundredth time, you should question your old assumptions and see if you can work almost exclusively on this "magical" device. Not just for the sake of being different—though Aiden would definitely argue it looks great on camera. 

The `#MainNostalgiaComputer2Go` challenge will force you to simplify your work, strip away the bloat, and make your daily routine infinitely more rewarding and fun. It's a fresh start, and honestly, it's simply amazing. 

Think of this book as your ultimate blueprint. In the coming chapters, we'll walk you through what our `#MainNostalgiaComputer2Go` transition actually looked like—from the messy reality to the breakthroughs. We'll show you what we changed, what we tweaked, and what we threw out. 

Along the way, Lavar will probably chime in with some wildly unhelpful travel logistics, Lloyde will run by shirtless making weird sound effects to keep you from taking yourself too seriously, and Aiden will try to frame the whole thing like a Tarantino indie film shot on his reliable 2020 Samsung Galaxy M31. But behind the laughs, we'll demonstrate precisely which apps, services, and accessories expedited our transition into this uncharted, slightly strange, and entirely rewarding new world. 

Buckle up—it's going to be a strange, hilarious, but truly great ride.

## This Is My Open-Source Book

The roots of this book go way back, to the summer of 2017, right after the monumental iOS 11 announcement. Today, I'm writing and revising these very words on the iPad Pro I own.

And yet, even though the original ideas were hammered out years ago—and both hardware and software have evolved in wild, unexpected directions—we still believe the heart of this book is just as relevant now as it was back then.

Too many people still look at iPads as oversized, inferior toys, claiming you can't possibly get "real work" done on a tablet. Meanwhile, Lloyde is probably in the background running a bit with his shirt off, making weird sound effects, while Aiden—quietly nursing his ambitions of becoming the next Quentin Tarantino and clutching his reliable, trusty Samsung Galaxy M31 from 2020 that he's had since 2021—just shakes his head at us.

That’s why we decided to make this book **open-source**. We’ve put it right here on the internet for everyone to read, critique, and enjoy. Because this is the absolute beginning of the book, it will continuously go through revisions, evolving right alongside our workflows. We're keeping it open to show you firsthand just how liberating, creative, and exciting work and play on a legacy iPad can truly be.

Let's go!
